# RC15 Resources — Editorial Guidelines

These guidelines apply to new tutorials and substantial revisions across the permanent RC15 Resources library. Use them alongside the technical requirements of each subject.

## Writing

Write in clear, natural British English for an international audience of architecture and urban design students. Assume curiosity, not prior programming knowledge.

Be economical with words without becoming abrupt. Use connected paragraphs, but remove repetition, filler, needless qualifications and sentences that only restate a point. Prefer concrete examples to abstract claims. Avoid generic enthusiasm and ceremonial introductions.

Introduce unfamiliar concepts before using them. Explain technical detail when it helps understanding, and do not sacrifice accuracy for simplicity. Use headings for meaningful stages of the tutorial rather than every small idea.

## Narrative and teaching

Start with a recognisable problem or a compelling possibility. Develop the explanation in a deliberate order, so that each section prepares the next.

Explain *why* a technique works, not just the sequence of clicks. Use practical architectural or urban-design exercises that allow experimentation and produce meaningful results. Let illustrations demonstrate comparisons and outcomes rather than repeating the surrounding text.

Distinguish verified steps from assumptions or experiments. Include software versions, limitations, sources and troubleshooting where they affect reproducibility. Do not present untested code or workflows as tested.

## Reviewing a tutorial

Read the **whole document** before making substantial changes. Check its argument, order and rhythm. If repeated small edits have made the text uneven, rewrite the affected passage as a coherent whole instead of patching individual sentences.

Before committing, read the complete revision again and remove needless transitions, duplications and sections that do not contribute to the learning outcome. Check examples, image paths and links. Keep year-specific teaching details out of the permanent resource library.
